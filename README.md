## Introduction

A microwave test bench is an assembly of microwave components held together by nuts and bolts. A microwave source (oscillator) sits at one end; the generated waves travel down a waveguide through the various components so that the propagation of the waves and their interaction/processing by each component can be observed.

## 1. Klystron Power Supply

<img width="271" height="215" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3f1f0c79-da13-4824-a32c-5945c0f3e361" />

A regulated supply for operating low-power klystrons. It generates the voltages needed to drive reflex klystron tubes such as the 2K25, 2K56 and 2K22. The supply is stable, regulated and short-circuit protected, allows the beam voltage to be varied continuously, and has built-in square-wave and saw-tooth generators for amplitude and frequency modulation.

## 2. Reflex Klystron (Klystron Mount with Tube)

<img width="218" height="223" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/88a6c65e-7df5-4737-963a-3e07f78e56b3" />

A waveguide section of suitable length carrying an octal base on its broad wall for mounting the klystron tube. A movable short at one end directs the microwave energy generated by the tube. A small hole at the exact centre of the broad wall takes the coupling pin of the tube, since the electric field vector of the EM energy is maximum at the centre. Maximum power transfer is obtained by tuning the movable plunger.

### The Reflex Klystron

<img width="414" height="330" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/28cdf56f-44e6-41d7-9d47-38be61833b3a" />

The anode of the klystron is a resonant cavity containing perforated grids that let accelerated electrons pass through. Instead of being collected by a positive electrode, these electrons are deflected by a negatively biased *reflector* and fall back into the cavity grids. If they return at the right instant they reinforce the oscillatory electric field across the grids, and oscillations are excited and sustained in the cavity. Power is coupled out either through a loop (coaxial output) or through an aperture (waveguide output).

Once the electrons give up their kinetic energy to the oscillatory field, the spent electrons fall back to the positively biased control grid and add to the control-grid current. When oscillations are sustained, the falling electrons yield most of their energy to the cavity field and are collected by the cavity grids; because spent electrons no longer reach the control grid, a pronounced dip in control-grid current accompanies the onset of oscillation.

## 3. Isolator

<img width="242" height="238" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8a350772-9008-4070-bf80-2dcf1aab02d3" />

A two-port device that transmits microwave/RF power in one direction only. Together with the attenuator it stops reflected power from reaching the oscillator and pulling the cavity or Gunn diode off tune when the load impedance is varied. An ideal isolator passes all power from port 1 to port 2 while absorbing all power entering port 2.

It is a non-reciprocal device with a non-symmetric scattering matrix:

```
      | 0  0 |
S =   | 1  0 |
```

Non-reciprocity requires a non-reciprocal material — at microwave frequencies invariably a ferrite biased by a static magnetic field. The ferrite is placed so the microwave signal presents it with a rotating magnetic field whose rotation axis is aligned with the bias field; the ferrite responds differently to the two senses of rotation, so signals travelling in opposite directions are treated differently (phase-shifted, displaced or absorbed).

## 5. Attenuator

<img width="247" height="208" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/771123ea-bafb-4292-9bed-3db7e9f371e7" />

Attenuators adjust the power flowing in a waveguide and come in fixed, variable and rotary-vane types.

* **Fixed** — any fixed attenuation between 3 dB and 40 dB, calibrated over a frequency band.
* **Variable** — a convenient means of adjusting the power level very accurately.

## 6. Direct Reading Frequency Meter

<img width="226" height="214" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/40eb7f15-e14a-4367-b1d5-69671e66b066" />


A cylindrical cavity resonator with a variable short-circuit termination. The shorting plunger changes the cavity length and hence its resonant frequency. Readout is through long spiral dials with an overall accuracy of about 0.17 % (including dial calibration). The cavity produces a "suck-out" in the frequency response, so the knob is turned until a dip is observed in the output.

## 7. Slotted Line Section with Probe Carriage

<img width="349" height="260" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/82b573dc-e84b-41d5-a2b4-d6d62e8041e7" />

The basic instrument of microwave measurement — used to determine VSWR, attenuation, phase and impedance. It consists of a slotted waveguide, a travelling probe carriage and provision for attaching detecting instruments. The carriage position is read on a centimetre scale with a vernier of 0.1 mm least count, and the total travel exceeds three half-guide-wavelengths. The slot in the centre of the broad face does not radiate for the dominant mode. The section can also be used to measure reflection coefficient and return loss.

## 8. Crystal Detector

<img width="213" height="214" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3dbc4138-d800-4511-86e1-ff123474fad2" />

The detector mount is used to detect the microwave signal. An RF choke built into the crystal mounting reduces leakage from the BNC connector. At low microwave power levels the detector response approximates a square law, so it can be used with a high-gain selective amplifier having a square-law meter calibration.

## 9. VSWR Meter

<img width="292" height="177" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/bdad34e1-35c9-4d63-b328-61094c89db02" />

Measures the standing wave ratio on a transmission line, indicating the degree of mismatch between a line and its load (usually an antenna) and thus the effectiveness of impedance matching.

### Ways to express VSWR

The reflection coefficient Γ is what is read from a Smith chart. A magnitude of zero is a perfect match, a magnitude of one is perfect reflection. Γ is a vector, so it carries an angle, and unlike VSWR it distinguishes a short circuit (1 ∠180°) from an open circuit (1 ∠0°). Return loss is the magnitude of Γ expressed in decibels:

```
R.L. = −20 log |Γ|
```

## 10. Different Types of Antennas Available

### Conical Horn

<img width="226" height="194" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fcbdf4f1-5b7c-4b94-9810-82f24b11f4f8" />

Also called a waveguide-fed conical horn — a practical microwave antenna often used as a feed for communication/satellite dishes and radio telescopes. Its axial symmetry lets it handle any polarisation of the fundamental TE₁₁ mode, though the pin-fed design used here is linearly polarised. Variations that reduce diffraction effects, improve pattern symmetry and lower side lobes include corrugating the internal walls, curving the walls at the aperture, combining corrugations with wall curvature, and introducing higher-order modes to reduce the field at the aperture edges. A lens across the aperture compensates for phase error and narrows the beamwidth.

### Parabolic Dish

A parabolic antenna uses a reflector with the cross-sectional shape of a parabola to direct radio waves. It is highly directive — working like a searchlight reflector — and produces some of the highest gains and narrowest beamwidths of any antenna type. Applications include point-to-point radio, television and data communication, radar and radio astronomy, plus satellite TV (TVRO/DBS), terrestrial microwave links between cell sites and wireless WAN/LAN.

**Types of parabolic dish (by shape):**

* **Cylindrical** — curved in one direction only; the waves focus along a line, fed by a dipole on the focal line. Radiates a fan beam. With flat plates capping the curved ends it becomes a pillbox antenna.
* **Orange peel** — very long and narrow, shaped like a "C"; radiates an even wider fan beam and is often used for radar.
* **Paraboloidal or dish** — the most common type; radiates a narrow pencil beam along the axis.
* **Shrouded dish** — a cylindrical metal shield lined with absorbent material is attached to the rim to cut side and back lobes (back lobe reduction of about 10 dB), useful where several links share a frequency.

**Types by feed:**

<img width="270" height="407" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/4b9e41fd-5164-44b9-9fb0-816d48fc831d" />

* **Axial or front feed** — feed at the focus on the beam axis; the feed and its supports block part of the beam, limiting aperture efficiency to 55–60 %.
* **Offset or off-axis feed** — an asymmetrical segment of a paraboloid places the feed to one side, out of the beam path; widely used in home satellite dishes.
* **Cassegrain** — the feed is on or behind the dish and illuminates a convex hyperboloidal sub-reflector at the focus; keeps bulky feed electronics off the beam path. Aperture efficiency 65–70 %.
* **Gregorian** — like Cassegrain but with a concave (ellipsoidal) sub-reflector; over 70 % aperture efficiency.

## 11. Magic Tee

<img width="252" height="246" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/cf494478-1040-4995-8977-bb0d28116eae" />


A combination of E- and H-plane tees. Arm 3 forms an H-plane tee with arms 1 and 2; arm 4 forms an E-plane tee with arms 1 and 2. Arms 1 and 2 are the side or collinear arms. Port 3 is the H-plane port (sum port, P-port); port 4 is the E-plane port (difference port, Δ or S-port).

A signal injected into the H-plane port divides equally and **in phase** between ports 1 and 2. A signal injected into the E-plane port divides equally but **180° out of phase**. ## 13. Matched Termination

<img width="202" height="176" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9b06a4db-4010-4e2c-adf4-91a7fa26be2b" />

Used to terminate waveguide systems operating at low average power; designed to absorb all applied power and assure a low SWR. Used wherever a matched load is required — reflection measurements, discontinuity/obstacle measurements — and as a precise reference load with tee junctions and directional couplers.
